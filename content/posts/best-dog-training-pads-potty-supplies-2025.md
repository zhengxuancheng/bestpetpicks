## What to Look For in Dog Training Pads

### Absorption Speed and Leak Protection

A good pad locks in liquid within seconds so your dog doesn't track wet paws across the floor. The best pads have five to eight layers, including a waterproof backing and a polymer core that turns liquid into gel. Leakproof edges are important too — some pads leak at the seams when a heavy dog pees near the border.

### Size and Coverage

Standard pads measure 22 x 22 inches, which works for most small and medium dogs. Large dogs need jumbo pads (30 x 30 inches) or you end up with misses. For crate training, get pads sized to fit the tray. Some brands offer multi-size packs so you can test what works.

### Attractant vs. Unscented

Many pads use herbal attractant scents to encourage dogs to use them. They work well for puppies but some dogs develop a preference for that specific scent and refuse un-scented pads later. Unscented pads are better for transitioning to outdoor potty training. For senior dogs or long-term indoor use, unscented heavy-duty pads are the safer bet.

### Reusable vs. Disposable

Washable pads cost more upfront but save money over time if you're potty training for months. Disposable pads are convenient and you just toss them, but the costs add up. For puppies, disposables are less hassle during the intense phase. For senior dogs with incontinence, washable pads on furniture work better.

## FAQ

### At what age can I start using potty training pads?

Puppies can start using training pads as early as 8 weeks old. By that age they can stand and sniff, but don't have full bladder control — especially small breeds. Put the pad in a consistent spot and take them to it every hour, after meals, and after naps. It takes most puppies 4-6 months to be fully reliable with pads.

### How often should I change dog training pads?

Change disposable pads after every 1-2 uses. Dogs don't like standing in wet pads and may refuse to reuse them. For heavy-drinking breeds or long periods, change after each use. Washable pads can handle 3-4 accidents before needing a wash. A wet or saturated pad that isn't changed defeats the purpose of potty training.

### Do reusable pads smell after washing?

Quality washable pads with a waterproof PUL layer don't retain odors if cleaned properly. Use hot water, a pet-safe enzymatic detergent, and add a half cup of white vinegar to the rinse cycle. Avoid fabric softener — it coats the absorbent layers and reduces performance. Air drying in sunlight helps remove any lingering smells.

### Can I use training pads for a senior dog with incontinence?

Yes. Heavy-duty washable pads are ideal for senior dogs with incontinence. They handle larger volumes and cost less over time than disposables. Place pads on sofas, beds, and favorite sleeping spots. For dogs that spend most of the day on one floor, consider a washable pad over a large area like a 24 x 36 inch pad or a reusable pee pad in their bed area.

### How do I transition my dog from pads to outdoor potty?

Gradually move the pad closer to the door over 7-10 days, then just outside the door, then to the yard. At the same time, take the dog outside frequently — every 30-60 minutes at first. A real grass pad like Paws & Pals helps bridge the transition because the dog is already used to peeing on grass. Expect some accidents during the transition period.
