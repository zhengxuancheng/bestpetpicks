A dog in the car is great company. A dog shedding, drooling, and tracking mud across your back seat is less great. Dog car seat covers and hammocks solve that problem. They create a barrier between your pet and your upholstery, catch the dirt and hair, and keep your seats from ending up scratched.

I tested seven dog car seat covers and hammocks. Bench seats, bucket seats, hatchbacks, trucks with bench fronts. Some fit perfectly. Some shifted around after two blocks. Some claim to be waterproof but aren't. The ones below are the ones I'd actually put in my car.

## What to Look For in a Dog Car Seat Cover

### Hammock vs. Bench Cover vs. Bucket Cover

Three main types exist, and the right one depends on your car and your dog. **Hammock-style covers** attach to the front and rear headrests, creating a suspended barrier that blocks the gap between the back seat and the front seats. This stops your dog from climbing into the front and catches debris that would otherwise fall into the footwell. Best for dogs that like to roam and for bench-style back seats.

**Bench covers** lay flat over the back seat and attach to the headrests. They don't block the front gap, so a determined dog can still climb through, but they're simpler, lighter, and easier to fold up. Best for small-to-medium dogs that stay put.

**Bucket covers** fit individual bucket seats and usually include side flaps that protect the door panels from scratches. Best for trucks with bench fronts, two-door cars, or SUVs with split-folding rear seats where you still need access to one seat.

### Waterproofing: Coating vs. Membrane vs. Liner

This is the make-or-break feature. **Polyester with PVC orTPU coating** is the most common — it's affordable, reasonably waterproof, and easy to wipe clean. The coating wears off after 6-12 months of regular use and washing, at which point the cover starts leaking through.

**Multi-layer quilted covers** sandwich a waterproof membrane (usually PU or TPU) between layers of fabric. They're thicker, more comfortable for the dog, and the waterproofing lasts longer because the membrane isn't exposed. The tradeoff is weight and bulk.

**Removable waterproof liners** are the best design. The top layer is quilted fabric for comfort while the bottom layer is a separate waterproof liner that you can remove and wash. If the liner wears out, you replace just the liner instead of the whole cover.

Look for 3000mm+ hydrostatic head rating on the waterproof layer — anything less will leak under pressure (like a dog sitting on it for an hour).

### Anchor System: What Keeps It in Place

A cover that shifts every time your dog moves is worse than no cover at all. The best systems use **seat anchors** (metal or heavy-duty plastic clips that slot into the seat crease and hook under the seat), **headrest straps** (adjustable straps with quick-release buckles around the front and rear headrests), and **anti-slip backing** (rubberized dots or mesh on the underside that grips the seat fabric).

Covers with only headrest straps shift on leather or pleather seats. Covers with seat anchors plus headrest straps stay put. Anti-slip backing helps but isn't sufficient on its own.

### Size and Coverage

Standard hammock covers fit most sedans and SUVs. Extended-length hammocks reach from the back of the front seats to the back of the rear seats — important for larger dogs or deeper vehicles like SUVs with the rear seats folded down. Some covers include side curtain protectors that wrap around the door panels. These are essential if your dog hangs their head out the window (scratched door cards are permanent).

### Washability

You will wash this thing. If the cover is machine-washable (cold water, gentle cycle), it's a winner. If the backing peels or disintegrates in the wash, it's not. Covers with a sewn-in waterproof membrane can trap water and take two dryer cycles to dry. Covers with a removable liner dry in one. Quick-dry fabric covers (like outdoor gear) are the most convenient — hose them off, wipe down, and they're dry in an hour.

## FAQ

### Will a hammock cover work in a pickup truck with a bench front seat?

Most hammock covers are designed for the rear seat of a sedan or SUV and don't fit truck front benches. The OmiPet Truck Cover is purpose-built for this use case. If you don't want a full wrap, the BarksBar will fit a truck bench front seat if it's the large size, but the side flaps won't wrap around the door panels properly. Measure your bench width before ordering — not all truck benches are the same size.

### Can I wash my dog car seat cover in a washing machine?

Most covers labeled machine-washable can go in a front-loader on cold, gentle cycle. Top-loaders with an agitator can damage straps and buckles — put the cover in a mesh laundry bag. Never use fabric softener, which degrades waterproof coatings. Air drying is best for most covers. The BarksBar is the only one in this review that's machine-dryable (low heat). Covers with sewn-in waterproof membranes take 6-12 hours to air dry.

### How do I keep my dog's seat cover from sliding on leather seats?

Leather seats are the hardest surface to anchor a cover. Look for covers with seat anchors (not just headrest straps), anti-slip rubberized backing, and weight. Heavy covers with seat anchors and anti-slip backing stay put. Light covers with only headrest straps will slide. The 4Knines and Plush Paws have the best grip on leather.

### Can I use a dog seat cover with child car seats?

Yes, but you need a cover with a center zipper or pass-through for the car seat tether. The Plush Paws Quilted Hammock has a zippered center section that creates a pass-through. Most bench covers without this feature block the LATCH anchors. If you need both dog and child seats in the back, look specifically for covers that mention car seat compatibility.

### Are back seat dog covers safe for crash-tested barriers?

No. A fabric seat cover is not a crash barrier. In a collision, a 50-pound dog becomes a 1500-pound projectile. Seat covers contain your dog's mess, not your dog. For crash protection, you need a crash-tested crate or a safety harness with a seatbelt tether (like the Sleepypod Clickit or Ruffwear Load Up). If you value your dog's safety in a crash, buy both a cover and a harness.
