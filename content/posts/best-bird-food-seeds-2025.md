A parakeet will eat almost anything you put in front of it, which is exactly why so many birds end up on a diet of straight seed and nothing else. Seed is high in fat, it tastes great, and a bird given the choice will pick it every single time. Left to their own devices, most pet birds would eat like a teenager at a state fair. That's the core problem this article is trying to solve.

I've kept budgies, cockatiels, and one very opinionated conure over the years, and I've cycled through most of the bird food on the market. For this roundup I ran six of the most popular foods past a budgie and a cockatiel over three months. ## What to Look For in Bird Food

### Seeds vs Pellets

Seed mixes are what most birds grow up on, and they have one big advantage: birds love them. The downside is nutrition. Most seed is heavy on fat and light on vitamins, and a bird that eats only seed misses out on calcium, amino acids, and a dozen other things that matter over a long life. Pellets were invented to fix that. They're uniform, nutritionally complete, and a bird can't pick around them. Vets almost always push pellets, and they're right to, but a sudden all-pellet switch will earn you a hunger strike. The practical answer for most owners is a pellet base with a little seed mixed in, or a fortified seed mix like the ones below.

### Ingredients and Fillers

Read the ingredient list the way you'd read your own food label. The first ingredients should be named grains, seeds, or meals. What you don't want is a bag that's mostly filler: red millet dust, crushed hulls, and artificial colors that exist to make the mix look healthy. Cheap grocery store mixes are often half filler. Your bird cracks the good seeds, drops the rest, and you end up with a ring of empty husks around the cage and a bird that actually ate maybe a third of the bag. You're not saving money at that point, you're throwing it away.

### Match the Food to Your Bird

Size matters more than people think. A budgie needs small seed and small pellets that fit its beak. A cockatiel steps up a size. A conure or an African grey needs larger pellets, nuts, and chunkier pieces. Feed a cockatiel food to a budgie and the seed is too big to crack comfortably. Feed budgie food to a conure and they'll bulldoze through it in a day. Every food below lists which birds it's sized for, so match the bird first and the price second.

### Switching a Picky Eater

Birds are creatures of habit, and a bird that has eaten seed for years will treat a bowl of pellets like a foreign object. The trick is to switch gradually over two to three weeks, mixing the new food into the old in increasing amounts. Weigh your bird weekly during the switch. Weight is the honest signal: a bird that's losing weight isn't eating, and you need to slow the transition down. A bird that holds steady is doing fine, even if it glares at you for a while.

### Storage and Freshness

Bird seed goes rancid. The oils in sunflower and safflower turn, and old seed loses most of its nutritional value long before it looks bad. Store food in an airtight container in a cool, dry spot, and buy bags sized to what your bird actually eats in a month. If you've ever seen tiny moths in the pantry, they came from somewhere, and an open bag of seed is a prime suspect. A sealed bin ends that problem.

## FAQ

### Should I feed my bird seeds or pellets?

Pellets, ideally, because they're nutritionally complete and a bird can't pick around them. But the best diet is the one your bird will actually eat. If your bird is a lifelong seed eater, start with a fortified seed mix and work toward pellets gradually. A bird that eats well is healthier than a bird on a theoretically perfect diet that it refuses.

### How do I switch a seed-addicted bird to pellets?

Mix the new pellets into the old seed in increasing proportions over two to three weeks. Start at about 25 percent pellets and work up. Weigh your bird weekly; if it's losing weight, slow down the transition. Some birds convert in a week, some take a month. Foraging foods like Nutri-Berries can help bridge the gap.

### How much should my parakeet eat each day?

A parakeet eats roughly one to two tablespoons of food per day, though it varies with the bird and the food. The rule that matters more: a bird should never have an empty food bowl for more than a few hours. Refill daily, remove husks and dust, and watch the weight. Appetite changes are often the first sign of illness.

### Do birds need variety, or can I feed one food?

A complete pellet is designed to be a sole diet, so one good pellet is fine every day. Seed mixes are not complete, so they benefit from variety and from supplemental vegetables like broccoli, carrot, and leafy greens. Fresh veg daily is a habit worth building regardless of what you feed.

### How do I keep bird seed fresh?

Store it in an airtight container in a cool, dry place, and buy bags sized to a month of feeding. Seed oils go rancid over time, and stale seed loses nutrition and attracts pantry moths. If the seed smells sour or you see webs or moths, throw it out and start fresh.
