## What to Look For in a Pet Ramp or Stair

### Weight Capacity

This is the first filter. A ramp for a 10-pound cat is a different product than one for a 80-pound Labrador. Most ramps support 50–150 lbs, but heavy-duty models go up to 250+ lbs. Always buy for the heaviest pet who will use it. A ramp that flexes under your dog's weight is worse than no ramp — it teaches them the ramp isn't safe.

### Surface Traction

Pets need to feel their paws grip. Look for carpeted surfaces (most common), rubber treads, or foam with a textured overlay. Carpet is familiar and comfortable but collects hair. Rubber treads are easier to clean and grip well even with wet paws. Avoid smooth plastic surfaces — they're a fall waiting to happen.

### Height and Rise

Measure the height of the surface your pet needs to reach. A standard bed is about 25 inches. An SUV cargo floor is 28-32 inches. A sofa is 18-22 inches. The ramp or stairs needs to reach that height at a slope your pet can manage. A good rule: the gentler the incline, the easier it is for pets with mobility issues.

### Stability and Anti-Slip Base

The ramp should not slide forward when your pet walks up it. Look for rubber feet, non-slip pads, or a design that braces against the furniture. Folding ramps with rubberized feet are the most stable. Stairs generally need a wide base to prevent tipping.

### Portability and Storage

If you're using the ramp for the car, it needs to fold or telescope compactly. For home use on the bed or couch, a lightweight option you can move easily matters more than foldability. Some ramps fold to the size of a large laptop — ideal for travel.

## FAQ

### Do most pets actually use ramps, or do they avoid them?

Most pets take to ramps with a little encouragement. Start by placing the ramp flat on the floor with treats scattered on it so they learn to walk on it without fear. Then gradually raise the angle. Cats usually adapt faster than dogs. Small dogs adapt faster than large breeds who are used to jumping. Give it a week.

### Ramps vs. stairs — which is better?

Ramps are generally better for pets with mobility issues (arthritis, hip dysplasia, post-surgery recovery) because the continuous surface allows them to walk at their own pace without lifting their paws to a new step. Stairs are better for small dogs and cats who find steps more intuitive, and they take up less floor space.

### How do I clean a pet ramp?

Carpet-covered ramps need vacuuming and occasional spot cleaning. Most removable covers can be machine-washed on gentle cycle. Solvit and PetSTEP offer replacement covers if the original wears out. Rubber-tread ramps wipe clean with a damp cloth.

### Can a pet ramp be used outdoors?

Most ramps are designed for indoor use. The WeatherTech ramp and Pet Loader are the exceptions — their aluminum frames and carpet materials hold up to outdoor conditions, though they shouldn't be left in the rain. Indoor carpet ramps absorb moisture and can develop mildew.

### What's the right ramp length for my bed or car?

Measure the height from floor to the top of the surface. For a standard 25-inch bed, a 60-inch ramp creates about a 25-degree incline. For an SUV cargo floor at 30 inches, a 75-inch ramp is ideal. The longer the ramp relative to the height, the gentler the slope.
