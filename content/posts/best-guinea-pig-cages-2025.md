Guinea pigs are not hamsters. They need floor space, not height — these guys are ground-dwellers who need room to run popcorning laps across a wide, open area. A proper cage makes the difference between a bored, stressed guinea pig and one that wheeks at you every time the fridge opens.

I tested 8 cages — budget starters, store-bought habitats, and full C&C custom builds. Some are genuinely worth the money. Others are basically hamster cages with misleading labels.

## What to Look for in a Guinea Pig Cage

Here are the factors that actually separate a good cage from a bad one.

### Minimum Size Requirements

The Humane Society recommends **7.5 square feet** for one guinea pig and **10.5 square feet** for two. Anything smaller and you'll see stress behaviors — bar chewing, hiding, or fighting in bonded pairs. The gold standard is a 2×4 C&C cage (about 13 square feet), which comfortably houses a pair.

If you're looking at pet store cages labeled "guinea pig habitat," check the actual floor dimensions. Many of them are sized for hamsters and sold with misleading labels.

### C&C (Cubes and Coroplast) vs. Store-Bought

C&C cages — modular wire grid cubes with a corrugated plastic base — are the gold standard in the guinea pig community. They're spacious, customizable, and significantly cheaper than comparably sized store-bought cages. The downsides: you assemble them yourself, and they're not as visually polished as a single-piece enclosure.

Store-bought cages (Midwest, Prevue, Ferplast) are easier to set up and look nicer in a living room. They're also usually smaller and more expensive per square foot than a DIY C&C.

### Accessibility

A cage with a full-open top or large front doors makes daily cleaning much easier. Guinea pigs need spot-cleaning every day or two — if reaching the far corner requires a full-arm stretch through a small door, you'll dread doing it. Look for full open tops, drop-down doors, or removable lids.

### Floor Protection

Your floors will thank you for a cage with a solid base pan or a waterproof layer beneath the fleece. Coroplast bases (plastic corrugated sheet) are the most common for C&C cages — they're waterproof, easy to wipe down, and cheap to replace.

### Bar Spacing

For guinea pigs, bar spacing should be **1 inch or less**. Anything wider and even an adult guinea pig can squeeze through or get its head stuck. Baby guinea pigs may need 1/2-inch spacing or a solid-walled cage.

## FAQ

### How much space do two guinea pigs need?

At minimum, 10.5 square feet of continuous floor space. A 2×4 C&C cage (13 sq ft) is the community standard for a bonded pair. You can go larger — 2×5 or 3×4 — and your guinea pigs will use every inch running laps.

### Can I use a hamster cage for a guinea pig?

Almost never. Hamster cages are built for climbers and burrowers, not ground-dwelling guinea pigs. Most hamster cages have tubes, small platforms, and less than 3 square feet of floor space — all wrong for guinea pigs. Stick to cages specifically designed for them.

### What's the best bedding for guinea pig cages?

Fleece liners are the most popular choice in the guinea pig community — they're reusable, soft, and easy to spot-clean. Paper-based bedding (Carefresh, Kaytee Clean & Cozy) is a good alternative if you prefer disposable bedding. Avoid cedar and pine shavings — the phenols in their oils can cause respiratory issues.

### Do guinea pigs need a top on their cage?

Not if you have no other pets or small children who might reach in. Guinea pigs don't climb or jump out of enclosures with 12-inch+ walls. But if you have cats, dogs, or toddlers, a secure mesh lid is essential.

### How often should I clean a guinea pig cage?

Spot-clean daily — remove wet spots in the fleece, soiled bedding, and leftover fresh vegetables. Full bedding change every 5-7 days for fleece, 7-10 days for paper bedding. C&C cages with large coroplast bases are easier to clean because you can access every corner easily.
