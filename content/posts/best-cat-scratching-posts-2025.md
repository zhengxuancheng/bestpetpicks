Scratching is a natural, instinctive behavior for cats — it stretches their muscles, sharpens their claws, and marks their territory. Without a proper scratching post, your sofa, carpet, and doorframes become the target. The right scratching post redirects this behavior and keeps both your cat and your furniture happy. In this guide, we break down the best cat scratching posts available right now, from tall sisal towers to wall-mounted designs and multi-function cat trees.

## What to Look for in a Cat Scratching Post

Before we get to the picks, here are the key factors to consider.

### Height and Stability

Cats need to stretch their full body length while scratching. For an adult cat, a post should be at least **28 to 32 inches tall**. Anything shorter, and your cat won't get the deep stretch they crave — so they'll look elsewhere. Equally important is stability: a wobbly post will scare a cat away permanently. Look for **wide, heavy bases** (at least 16 × 16 inches) and solid wood or MDF construction.

### Scratching Surface Material

Sisal rope is the gold standard. It's durable, satisfying to scratch, and lasts years with proper use. Corrugated cardboard scratchers are cheaper and loved by many cats, but they need replacing every few months. Carpet-covered posts are common at budget prices, but **avoid them if possible** — cats can catch their claws in the loops, and carpet texture encourages scratching your actual carpet. Some premium posts use sisal fabric panels for a smoother shred.

### Design Type

Vertical posts mimic tree trunks and suit cats who scratch with their paws up. Horizontal cardboard loungers work for cats who prefer scratching the floor — some cats have a strong preference for one orientation. Wall-mounted posts save floor space and can be installed at custom heights. Cat trees with integrated scratching surfaces offer climbing, perching, and scratching in one unit. Choose based on your cat's scratching habits and your available space.

### Multi-Cat Durability

If you have multiple cats, the post will see 2–3x the wear. Look for **replaceable sisal wraps**, extra-thick sisal rope (at least 0.37 inches / 9.5mm diameter), and heavy bases that won't tip when two cats scratch simultaneously. Some premium brands sell replacement parts, extending the post's life significantly.

## FAQ

### How tall should a scratching post be?

**At least 28 to 32 inches** for an average adult cat. Your cat should be able to stand on their hind legs, extend their front paws fully upward, and scratch without hitting the top of the post. For large breeds like Maine Coons, aim for 34 to 36 inches. Posts that are too short lead cats to seek out taller alternatives — like your door frames.

### My cat ignores the scratching post. What can I do?

Placement is critical. Put the post near where your cat already scratches, near a favorite sleeping spot, or in a high-traffic social area — cats scratch to mark territory, not in hidden corners. Rub catnip on the sisal, use a wand toy to drag along the post to trigger interest, and reward any interaction with treats. Avoid forcing your cat onto the post; let them discover it naturally. If they still ignore it, try a different material (cardboard instead of sisal) or orientation (horizontal instead of vertical).

### How often should I replace a cat scratching post?

A quality sisal post (like the SmartCat) can last **2 to 4 years** with a single cat. Cardboard scratchers typically last 3 to 8 months depending on use. Replace a post when the sisal is shredded through to the core, the post wobbles significantly, or your cat loses interest. Pro tip: rotate the post occasionally — most cats focus on one side, and turning it extends usable life.

### Can multiple cats share one scratching post?

Yes, but with caveats. In multi-cat households, provide **at least one scratching surface per cat, plus one extra**, placed in different locations. Cats scratch to mark territory, and forcing them to share a single post can create stress. The FEANDREA cat tree or a combination of a SmartCat vertical post and a PetFusion cardboard lounger in separate rooms works well for two-cat homes.

### Are carpet-covered scratching posts a bad idea?

Generally, yes. Carpet posts teach cats that scratching carpet-textured surfaces is acceptable — which extends to your actual carpet. Carpet loops can also snag claws painfully. Sisal and cardboard provide a clear texture distinction from household fabrics. If you already own a carpet post and your cat uses it without issues, it's fine to keep — but don't buy a new one.
