Some pets have a talent for being where they are not supposed to be. The kitchen while you are cooking. The stairs when the front door opens. The couch cushions right after you fluff them. A good pet gate or playpen solves the boundary problem without turning your home into a prison.

We tested 6 pet gates and playpens on dogs, cats, and the occasional determined rabbit. Pressure-mount, hardware-mount, freestanding, and modular designs — we put them all through the same tests.

## What to Look For in a Pet Gate or Playpen

### Mount Type: Pressure vs. Hardware vs. Freestanding

Pressure-mount gates use tension against door frames or walls — no drilling, no holes, easy to move. They are fine for everyday containment of calm pets but will not hold a dog that body-slams the gate when the delivery truck passes. Hardware-mount gates screw into the wall or door frame. They are dramatically more secure but leave holes. Freestanding playpens sit on the floor with no wall attachment — great for indoor or outdoor use but easier for a determined jumper to escape.

For stairs, always use a hardware-mount gate. A pressure-mount gate at the top of the stairs is a fall risk every time a pet pushes against it.

### Gate Width and Adjustability

Standard doorways are 28 to 36 inches wide, but your door trim adds an inch or two. Most gates come with extension panels to fit wider openings — check the maximum width before buying. For extra-wide openings (48+ inches), look for gates specifically designed for that span. A gate that sits too loosely in the opening is a gate a pet will eventually dislodge.

### Swing Direction and Walk-Through

A walk-through gate with a door that swings both ways is far more convenient than one you have to climb over or unclip every time. Look for a gate with a one-handed latch mechanism — because the other hand will be holding a coffee mug or a squirming puppy. Some gates have a "hands-free" foot pedal latch that lets you nudge the gate open without bending over.

### Bar Spacing and Pet Size

Kittens and small-breed puppies can squeeze through gaps as small as 3 inches. Cats climb gates with horizontal bars. Dogs jump gates under 30 inches tall. Match the bar spacing, height, and configuration to the specific escape artist in your home. A gate that is perfect for a Labrador is a joke to a Jack Russell.

### Indoor vs. Outdoor Use

Wooden or painted gates belong indoors. Metal or weatherproof plastic gates can go outside. If you plan to use a playpen outdoors for supervised time, look for ground stakes or anchor points — a light playpen will skid across grass when a 50-pound dog leans against it.

## FAQ

### Are pressure-mount gates safe for the top of stairs?

No. Only use hardware-mount gates at the top of stairs. A pressure-mount gate can dislodge when a pet pushes against it, sending both the gate and the pet tumbling down the stairs. All major pet safety organizations recommend hardware-mount for any stair application. Pressure-mount gates are fine for doorways and hallways on a single floor.

### What height gate do I need to prevent my dog from jumping?

For most medium dogs (Labradors, Border Collies, Beagles), 30 inches is enough. For athletic breeds (Huskies, German Shepherds, Australian Shepherds), you need 36 inches or higher. Some Huskies can clear a 36-inch gate from a standing start. If you have a jumper, look for a playpen with a covered top rather than a gate, or use a hardware-mount gate at the top of a door frame where the dog cannot get a running start.

### Can I use a pet gate for cats?

Cats climb. Horizontal bar gates are a ladder to a cat. Vertical bar gates at 30+ inches are climbable but less appealing. For cat containment, look for gates that are at least 36 inches tall with vertical bars spaced 2 inches or closer. Even then, determined cats will find a way over. A better option for cats is a modular playpen with a top cover, or a screen door installation in the doorway.

### How do I measure my doorway for a pet gate?

Measure the width of the opening at its narrowest point (include the trim on both sides). Do not measure the door opening itself — the gate mounts against the trim, not the door. For pressure-mount gates, the pressure pads need to contact solid wood or drywall, not hollow trim. If your door frame is less than 1 inch deep, a pressure-mount gate may not have enough surface to grip securely.

### What is the best pet gate for extra-wide openings?

For openings over 48 inches, look for a hardware-mount gate designed for wide spans. The Cardinal Gates SS-30 is a popular choice for extra-wide entrances up to 72 inches. Alternatively, use a modular playpen with a gate panel to block the opening — the MidWest exercise pen can be configured as a wide barrier.

### How do I stop my dog from chewing the gate?

Spray the gate with a bitter apple deterrent (Grannick's Bitter Apple is the standard). Give the dog an alternative chew toy in the same area. If the dog is chewing the gate out of separation anxiety, the gate is a symptom, not the cause. Work on crate training or consult a behaviorist rather than buying a metal gate with chew-through warranty.
