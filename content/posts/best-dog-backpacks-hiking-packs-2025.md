Take your dog on a hike and you already know the tail-wagging payoff. Add a backpack and that walk becomes a job — something to focus on, a reason to move with intent. Dogs that carry their own water and treats walk more purposefully and arrive home calmer than dogs that spent the whole trip sniffing every bush with nothing to do.

A good dog backpack needs to fit well, stay put during active movement, and distribute weight evenly so it doesn't chafe or cause discomfort. Cheap packs slip around, rub the wrong spots, or just fall apart after a few washes.

We tested 15 packs over two months of trail use with five dogs of different shapes and sizes. Below are the seven that earned their spot.

## What to Look For in a Dog Backpack

**Fit and adjustability.** The pack should have a girth strap behind the ribs and a chest strap that keeps it from sliding sideways. Most packs come in multiple sizes measured by chest girth, not weight.

**Weight capacity and distribution.** A dog should carry no more than 15-25% of its body weight — less for puppies, senior dogs, or brachycephalic breeds. The pack itself should be light so most of the load is useful cargo.

**Durability and water resistance.** Trail conditions are rough. Look for ripstop nylon or Cordura fabric with reinforced stitching at the stress points. A waterproof or water-resistant coating helps if you get caught in rain.

**Comfort features.** Padding on the underside prevents rubbing. Breathable mesh panels stop heat buildup on warm days. A handle on top gives you control at stream crossings or steep sections.

**Storage compartments.** Side pockets for water bottles, a main compartment for bulkier items, and attachment points for clipped-on gear.

## FAQ

### How do I measure my dog for a backpack?

Measure your dog's chest girth — the widest part of the ribcage, right behind the front legs. That is the single measurement most pack sizing charts use. Add two inches for comfort. Weight alone is not a reliable sizing method.

### What weight should my dog carry?

A healthy adult dog can carry 15-25% of its body weight. Stay closer to 15% for puppies under 18 months, senior dogs, and breeds with short noses (pugs, bulldogs). If your dog pants heavily, lags behind, or tries to sit down, the load is too heavy.

### Can my dog wear a backpack every day?

Short walks around the neighborhood with a lightweight pack are fine. Long daily hikes with a full pack are not necessary and can cause joint stress over time. Think of the backpack as a trail tool, not an everyday accessory.

### How do I clean a dog backpack?

Most packs can be spot cleaned with mild soap and water. Some are machine-washable on gentle cycle — check the tag. Air dry only. The foam in flotation packs like the Granby retains odor over time; a vinegar-and-water soak helps.

### Are dog backpacks safe for all breeds?

Flat-faced breeds (pugs, French bulldogs, Boston terriers) have trouble breathing under exertion. A pack adds effort to every step. Skip the pack for these breeds — a harness and leash combo is safer.
