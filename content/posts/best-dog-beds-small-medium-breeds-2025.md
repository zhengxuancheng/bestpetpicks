Small and medium breed dogs have different needs from their larger cousins. A 15-pound terrier doesn't need the same bed as a 90-pound Labrador — the foam density, bolster height, and sleeping surface should match the dog's size and sleeping style. A bed that's too tall is hard for a small dog to climb into. A bed with soft foam designed for a 10-pound dog will bottom out under a 40-pound dog.

We tested 6 dog beds sized specifically for small and medium breeds (under 50 lbs), evaluating them on foam density, bolster support, washability, durability, and how well they match different sleeping positions — side sleepers, curl-up nappers, and sprawlers who take up more space than they should.

## What to Look For in a Dog Bed for Small and Medium Breeds

### Loft and Foam Density — Not All Orthopedic Foam Is Equal

The best orthopedic dog beds use high-density memory foam or egg-crate foam that supports the dog's weight without flattening. But density matters more for a 40-pound dog than a 10-pound one. Light dogs (under 15 lbs) do fine with softer foam or even polyfill beds. Dogs in the 20–50 lb range need at least 2 inches of high-density foam that won't compress permanently after a few months of use. Look for CertiPUR-US certified foam. That certification means the foam passed tests for durability, emissions, and heavy metals — not just marketing claims.

### Bolster Height — The Goldilocks Zone

Small breed dogs love bolsters (the raised edges of a bed) for leaning their head against while sleeping and for burrowing into. But a bolster that's too tall blocks their view and makes the bed feel like a sensory-deprivation tank. A good rule: the bolster should be about as tall as the dog is when it sits — roughly 5 to 8 inches for small breeds, 8 to 10 inches for medium breeds. Very small dogs (Chihuahuas, Yorkies) may prefer a flat bed with a lower rim they can climb over easily.

### Washability — The Most Important Feature Nobody Talks About

Every dog bed gets dirty. Every single one. If the bed doesn't have a machine-washable, removable cover, you will eventually be scrubbing it in a bathtub with a sponge and a sense of regret. Look for beds with zippered, removable covers that survive repeated machine washing without the zipper breaking or the fabric pilling. Bonus points for waterproof liners between the cover and foam core — they keep accidents, drool, and mud from soaking into the foam where you can't clean it.

### Sleeping Style Match

Dogs have distinct sleeping positions that should guide your bed choice:
- **Side sleepers and sprawlers** need a flat, open surface with minimal bolsters — the PetFusion and Big Barker NAP work well.
- **Curl-up sleepers** love donut or cuddler beds with raised rims they can nestle into — the Furhaven Donut excels here.
- **Leaners and head-resters** need bolsters — the Frisco Bolster and PetFusion are good picks.
- **Burrowers** want a cave or covered bed with a soft, dark interior.

## FAQ

### What size dog bed does my small breed dog need?

Measure your dog from nose to tail while they're sleeping in their preferred position. Add 4 to 6 inches to each dimension. If your dog stretches out, buy for that stretched length. If they curl up, measure the diameter of their curled position. A 15-pound dog that sprawls may need a 30-inch bed. A 15-pound curler may be fine with a 24-inch donut. When in doubt, go up a size — dogs use extra bed space to spread out as they shift positions through the night.

### Orthopedic foam vs. polyfill — which is better for my dog?

For dogs under 15 pounds, polyfill beds (like the Furhaven Donut) are perfectly adequate. The dog is light enough that the fill won't compress significantly over time, and the soft, moldable surface is comfortable. For dogs 15–50 lbs, orthopedic foam is worth the upgrade. The foam supports joints and distributes weight more evenly, reducing pressure on hips and elbows. Dogs with existing joint issues or dogs over 5 years old should always get orthopedic foam regardless of weight.

### How often should I wash my dog's bed?

Every 2 to 4 weeks for the cover, or whenever it's visibly dirty or smelly. If you wash only the cover, the foam core should be air-dried separately once a month. Beds without removable covers (like the Furhaven Donut) should be washed every 1 to 2 weeks since the entire bed goes through the machine. Wash in cold water on a gentle cycle, and NEVER put orthopedic foam in the washing machine — only the removable cover. Foam absorbs water like a sponge, takes days to dry, and will develop mold.

### Are heated dog beds safe for small breeds?

Yes, if used correctly. Beds with low-wattage (4W) thermostatically controlled pads are safe for continuous use. The K&H Thermo-Snuggly in this guide uses dual thermostats — a primary and a secondary safety cut-off — so it can't overheat. Risks are minimal. The main safety concern is cord chewing: if your dog is a chewer, don't leave them unsupervised with a heated bed. Use a fabric cord cover or replace the bed entirely with the cord inaccessible. Never put a heated bed in a crate without direct supervision — the dog can't move away from the heat if it gets too warm.
