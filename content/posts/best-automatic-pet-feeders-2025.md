An automatic pet feeder frees you from rigid feeding schedules, keeps portions consistent for weight management, and gives you peace of mind when you're working late or away for the day. The right feeder dispenses exactly the right amount at exactly the right time — every time. But with options ranging from simple $30 gravity feeders to $200 WiFi-connected smart stations, choosing the right one takes some thought. In this guide, we break down the best automatic pet feeders for cats and dogs, covering capacity, scheduling features, power backup, and real-world reliability.

## What to Look for in an Automatic Pet Feeder

Before we get to the picks, here are the key factors to consider.

### Feeder Type

Automatic feeders fall into three categories. **Gravity feeders** are the simplest — a hopper continuously refills the bowl as your pet eats, with no portion control. They're cheap but only suitable for pets who self-regulate their intake. **Programmable feeders** dispense set portions at scheduled times using a motorized mechanism — these are what most people mean by "automatic feeder." **Smart WiFi feeders** add app control, real-time notifications, camera feeds, and voice recording so you can monitor and feed your pet from anywhere.

### Capacity and Portion Control

Feeder capacity ranges from about 3 pounds (good for a cat's week) to 25+ pounds (suitable for large dogs or extended absences). Portion sizes vary too — some dispense in precise 1/8-cup increments, others in larger "meals" of 1/4 to 1/2 cup. If you're managing a pet's weight or administering precise amounts of prescription food, **fine-grained portion control is essential.** For free-fed cats, precision matters less.

### Power and Backup

The best feeders run on wall power for reliability but include **battery backup** so they don't miss a meal during a power outage. Some use batteries only (convenient for placement anywhere but requires regular battery changes). Check whether the feeder retains scheduling memory when power is lost — cheaper models may reset to factory defaults, leaving your pet hungry.

### Scheduling Flexibility

Look for feeders that support **multiple meals per day** (at least 4, ideally 6+), with customizable portion sizes for each meal. Some allow different schedules for weekdays versus weekends. WiFi-enabled models let you dispense extra meals remotely — handy if you're running late or want to give a bonus snack.

### Pet Type and Food Compatibility

Most automatic feeders work for both cats and dogs, but kibble size matters. Large-breed dog kibble can jam in dispensers designed for cat food. Some feeders struggle with mixed-shape or semi-moist foods. Check the **maximum kibble size** spec (typically 0.4 to 0.8 inches / 10–20mm) and verify your food fits. Very few automatic feeders handle wet food — the exceptions use ice packs or sealed compartments with single-meal rotation.

### Build Quality and Pet-Proofing

A determined Labrador can break into a cheap feeder in minutes. Look for sturdy construction, secure lids, and locking hoppers. For cat feeders, pet-proofing against a second pet is equally important — RFID-locked feeders open only for a specific cat's microchip or collar tag.

## FAQ

### Can automatic feeders dispense wet food?

Most cannot. The hopper-style feeders reviewed here are designed exclusively for dry kibble — wet food clogs mechanisms, spoils at room temperature, and can't be gravity-fed. The **Cat Mate C500** is the notable exception, using a rotating tray with ice packs to keep up to 5 wet meals fresh. For wet food beyond 5 meals, consider hiring a pet sitter or using a feeder with built-in refrigeration (significantly more expensive).

### What happens if the power goes out?

This depends on your feeder's design. Models with battery backup (WOPET, HoneyGuaridan, Petlibro, Arf Pets) continue dispensing on schedule during an outage. Some retain memory but don't dispense. Cheaper feeders may reset entirely, wiping your schedule and leaving your pet unfed. Always look for feeders with both wall power and battery backup if being home for every meal matters. For travel, test the backup function before you leave.

### How often do I need to refill the feeder?

A 7-liter (~29 cup) feeder lasts roughly **25–30 days for one cat** eating 1 cup per day, or **5–7 days for a large dog** eating 4 cups per day. Check the hopper weekly regardless — kibble can bridge or form voids that prevent dispensing even when the hopper looks full. A quick stir of the hopper during weekly checks prevents this.

### Can I use an automatic feeder with multiple pets?

Yes, but with caveats. In multi-pet homes where one pet steals food, a standard programmable feeder won't solve the problem — both pets can access the bowl when food dispenses. Solutions include: **microchip/RFID feeders** that open only for the assigned pet (not covered in this roundup — they're a separate product category), placing feeders in separate rooms, or using a covered feeding station with a microchip door. For pets that eat together peacefully, any of the feeders above work fine.

### Is a WiFi-connected feeder necessary?

Not for most people. A reliable programmable feeder like the PetSafe Simply Feed handles 99% of use cases — scheduled meals while you're at work, asleep, or away for the day. WiFi connectivity shines when you want to feed remotely (stuck in traffic, working late), receive confirmation that a meal was dispensed, or adjust schedules without walking to the feeder. It's a convenience, not a necessity, but the price gap between WiFi and non-WiFi feeders has narrowed to the point where the upgrade is often worth considering.

### Are automatic feeders safe for dogs that eat quickly and bloat?

Rapid eating is a risk factor for bloat (gastric dilatation-volvulus), especially in large, deep-chested breeds. The **PetSafe Simply Feed** addresses this with its slow-feed mode — the programmed meal dispenses in small amounts over 15 minutes. Other feeders that support many small meals throughout the day (rather than 1–2 large meals) also help. If your dog is a gulper, look for feeders with slow-feed options, or combine an automatic feeder with a slow-feed bowl attachment.
